READING THE RESULTS

Three distinctions that matter.

Canonical aggregate observations
01 / PRIMITIVES

Reading an edge isn’t
reasoning over a graph.

Jev reaches 100% adjacency accuracy, but 61.43% on degree and 58.75% on distance. Local success does not imply uniformly reliable structural decisions.

02 / CONSTRUCTION

How close is the solution
to the optimum?

Jev’s mean TSP gap is 45.85, versus 100.90 for Qwen3.5-27B, 183.70 for Qwen3.8-27B and 152.40 for Qwen2.5-72B. Each completes 40/40 episodes. These are absolute distance gaps, not percentages.

03 / SCALE & LIMITS

Bigger isn’t always better
at direct decisions.

Jev has smaller gaps on all four constructions than either added model. Yet Qwen3.5-27B beats Jev’s LT gap (0.425 vs 0.600), and Kev ties Jev. The older-generation 72B is not a scale-only control or a measure of general capacity.

What is actually being compared?

Twelve configurations use the same 960 canonical instances and identical initial logical requests. Constructions follow each model’s own history, not oracle prefixes. The 11,520 scheduled episode evaluations are not 11,520 independent graphs. “Jev-like” means open decision-oriented readouts, not verified reconstructions of Jev’s undisclosed architecture.

What changed in v14, and does Jev beat 72B?

Two completed full-BF16 configurations join the original ten: Qwen3.8-27B with thinking disabled and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct in direct-answer mode. Both use greedy legal-choice grammar, a 64-token ceiling and a 180-second deadline; each completes 960/960 episodes with 160/160 feasible constructions. Original results and retained failures are unchanged. Jev has smaller construction gaps than both additions, but 72B has slightly higher connectivity and distance accuracy (one answer out of 80 each). This is not universal superiority, parameter equivalence, a scale-only ablation, or a matched-hardware speed comparison.

What do the metrics mean?

Exact accuracy averages the five size-specific accuracies equally. For degree, this differs from pooling all 400 questions. Construction quality primarily uses mean per-episode objective gap: solution minus optimum for minimization (TSP), optimum minus solution for maximization (MaxCut, LT, modularity). Gaps are absolute, not percentages, and average only feasible completions. Units differ and must not be pooled. Feasible coverage is shown alongside every gap; a zero conditional gap does not imply zero failures. Optimum hits remain supplementary, with all scheduled cases in their denominator.

Are failures kept in the results?

Yes. GPT-5.4’s nine construction timeouts and Laya’s nine unsupported LT inputs remain in scheduled denominators. Unsupported inputs are not silently truncated. Laya’s constant-yes binary behavior is retained after native-score and mapping checks; it is a finding under this protocol, not a universal claim about the model.

Why not make the graphs harder for Astra?

The target is the capability profile of Jev and Jev-like systems. GPT models are upper reference points under different reasoning budgets. Making a task harder after direct models reach a low-score plateau may add little diagnostic value. Sizes 8–12 are descriptive strata, not proven capability thresholds.

What has — and hasn’t — been replicated?

A separate 952-instance prospective new-graph confirmation covers the original four configurations only. Later model extensions have not received that confirmation. Local registrations and within-project independent checks are not external preregistration or third-party replication. Taskwise point estimates do not establish family-wise significant rankings.

What is included in this website?

Only whitelisted task/size aggregate metrics, configuration descriptions and provenance hashes. No API keys, machine paths, prompts, raw responses, weights or hidden reasoning. The bundled paper is a working manuscript; this static presentation is not a full experimental replay package.
